liquid organic hydrogen carriers

From catalyst benchmarking to a 2.3 Nm3/h hydrogen-release reactor.

LOHCs store hydrogen in an ordinary liquid that moves through existing fuel infrastructure. The cost sits in the release step: dehydrogenation is endothermic, platinum-hungry, and hard to keep hot at scale.

At KIST I worked that chain end to end. Benchmarking studies across homocyclic carriers established which systems were worth pursuing on an energy-economic basis (Kwak et al., 2021), support-property studies fixed what the Pt/Al2O3 catalyst should sit on (Kwak et al., 2021), and atomic layer deposition on abundant oxide supports produced interfaces that hold up at low metal loading (Kwak et al., 2025).

Getting heat into the reactor was its own problem. A liquid-gas organic phase-change thermal management module (Kwak et al., 2021) carried the design to a scaled reactor producing 2.3 Nm3 h-1 of H2 (Kang et al., 2024) — work funded by Hyundai Motor Company and the National Research Foundation of Korea, and covered by issued US and Korean patents.

What I built. Catalyst synthesis and benchmarking, the phase-change thermal management module, and the scaled dehydrogenation reactor.
