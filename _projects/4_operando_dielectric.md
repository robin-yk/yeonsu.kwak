The recurring objection to microwave catalysis is that nobody can say what temperature the catalyst is at, so any "non-thermal" claim is unfalsifiable. Answering it requires measuring the material's dielectric response _during_ reaction, not before or after.

At the ITACA Institute (Universitat Politècnica de València) I developed perturbation-based **dual-mode cavity** protocols that quantify microwave heating profiles and dielectric properties under operando conditions. Combined with operando Raman, XRD, and catalytic measurements, this lets oxygen-vacancy-mediated reactivity in reducible oxides be tracked as the field drives it — dielectric response and redox state measured on the same sample, at the same time.

I coordinated the ITACA–UD co-development of the cavity, including its international shipment, and the preparation for XAS-compatible microwave experiments at the QAS beamline at Brookhaven National Laboratory. A manuscript on dielectric-redox coupling in reducible metal oxides is in preparation.

**What I built.** The dual-mode cavity measurement protocols, the operando dielectric/Raman workflow, and the beamline-compatible microwave cell.
