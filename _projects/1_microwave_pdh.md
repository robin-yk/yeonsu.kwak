Propane dehydrogenation is equilibrium-limited, strongly endothermic, and chronically fouled by coke. The industrial answer is a large H<sub>2</sub> co-feed and frequent regeneration — both of which cost propylene yield and energy.

Driving the reaction with microwaves changes the problem. Because the field deposits energy directly in the catalyst rather than through the reactor wall, the temperature the catalyst sees and the temperature the gas sees are no longer the same. On PtSn/SiO<sub>2</sub> this produced coke-resistant PDH **without any H<sub>2</sub> co-feed**, at higher energy efficiency and with markedly slower Pt nanoparticle sintering than an equivalent furnace-heated reactor {% cite kwak2023microwave %}.

The result was reproduced in an independent laboratory at INMA, University of Zaragoza, which mattered: microwave catalysis has a history of results that do not travel between rigs.

**What I built.** Continuous-flow single-mode microwave reactors with in-situ temperature and dielectric diagnostics, the PtSn/SiO<sub>2</sub> catalyst synthesis, and the cross-laboratory reproducibility protocol.
