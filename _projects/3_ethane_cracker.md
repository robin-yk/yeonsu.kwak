Steam cracking is one of the largest single sources of CO<sub>2</sub> in the chemical industry, and most of that carbon comes from the fired furnace rather than the chemistry.

Moving the heat source _inside_ the tube removes the furnace and the wall-to-gas temperature gradient with it. Working with CFD researchers, I led the experimental reactor development for an internally Joule-heated, short-contact-time ethane cracker: high temperature, milliseconds of residence time, and a radial temperature profile that no fired coil can produce. The design projects **higher ethylene yield with reduced capital requirements and CO<sub>2</sub> emissions** than conventional cracking {% cite mittal2025ethane %}.

**What I built.** The high-temperature internally heated reactor and its instrumentation, the cracking experiments used to anchor the CFD model, and the experimental case for the design.
