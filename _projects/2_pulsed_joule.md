Non-oxidative coupling of methane wants high temperature. So does coke. Holding a catalyst at that temperature continuously means paying for both.

Pulsed Joule heating decouples them. Short, high-amplitude thermal pulses reach the temperature the C–H activation step needs, then quench before the slower carbon-deposition and aromatization chemistry can run to completion. Paired with microkinetic modeling from collaborators, the experiments explained **why** the pulse train enhances coupling and suppresses coke and aromatic byproducts relative to steady heating at the same mean temperature — and let the methane coupling run in tandem with CO<sub>2</sub> reduction on the same catalyst bed {% cite kwak2025tandem %}.

The same pulsed approach intensifies steam methane reforming and water-gas shift in tandem {% cite railkar2025smr %}.

**What I built.** The pulsed Joule reactor and its transient thermal diagnostics, the tandem CH<sub>4</sub>/CO<sub>2</sub> experiments, and the experimental side of the mechanistic argument.
