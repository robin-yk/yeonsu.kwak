LOHCs store hydrogen in an ordinary liquid that moves through existing fuel infrastructure. The cost sits in the release step: dehydrogenation is endothermic, platinum-hungry, and hard to keep hot at scale.

At KIST I worked that chain end to end. Benchmarking studies across homocyclic carriers established which systems were worth pursuing on an energy-economic basis {% cite kwak2021benchmarking %}, support-property studies fixed what the Pt/Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> catalyst should sit on {% cite kwak2021support %}, and atomic layer deposition on abundant oxide supports produced interfaces that hold up at **low metal loading** {% cite kwak2025scalable %}.

Getting heat into the reactor was its own problem. A liquid-gas organic phase-change thermal management module {% cite kwak2021generator %} carried the design to a scaled reactor producing **2.3 Nm<sup>3</sup> h<sup>-1</sup> of H<sub>2</sub>** {% cite kang2024thermally %} — work funded by Hyundai Motor Company and the National Research Foundation of Korea, and covered by issued US and Korean patents.

**What I built.** Catalyst synthesis and benchmarking, the phase-change thermal management module, and the scaled dehydrogenation reactor.
